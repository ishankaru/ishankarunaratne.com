I was pulling systems apart to see how they broke long before anyone paid me to build them.

Professionally that is twenty-seven years now: networks, then servers, then databases, then architecture, then teams, and lately AI. The shape keeps changing and the underlying question never does, which is what happens when this breaks and who finds out first. I still write code most weeks, because a technology leader who has not touched the stack in three years is guessing, and the team can always tell.

How I work

Architecture

I design the shape of a system before anyone writes code: data model, failure modes, where the load lands, what happens at 3am when a dependency goes away. Then I stay close enough to the implementation to find out whether the design survived contact with reality.

Leadership

Technical strategy, hiring, and delivery, including M&A integrations and cloud migrations. I have taken teams through the unglamorous part, where two companies' systems have to become one without anybody's customers noticing.

Security

I came up reverse engineering and debugging at the instruction level, fascinated by how things break. That lens never left. Security is part of the architecture, decided early, not a hardening pass bolted on the week before launch.

AI, where it pays

LLM applications, retrieval-augmented generation, agents, and the evaluation harnesses that catch regressions before users do. The interesting question is rarely whether a model can do something. It is whether the output is reliable enough to put in front of a customer.
